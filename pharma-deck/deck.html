Strategy 1 · Psychology
FBI negotiation: from persuasion to reverse negotiation
Step 1
Accusation audit
Voice their objections first
Step 2
A “safe no”
The client feels in control
Step 3
Calibrated questions
Open “how” questions invite collaboration
Outcome
They solve it
The client works out the answer themselves
Accusation audit · opening line
“Doctor, you probably think I’m here to repeat the same scientific pitch you hear every day, and that our product is priced higher without justification.”
Impact: raising the objection first neutralises their defensive posture.
Closed question · removed
“Will you prescribe our product?”
Calibrated · open “how” questions
“How can I support you in cases that don’t respond to first-line treatment?”
“How can we overcome the price barrier for the patient without affecting treatment quality?”

Strategy 2 · Finance
Pharmonomics: speak the pharmacy’s financial language
Shelf return, not simple margin
GMROI= Gross marginAverage inventory cost
Gross Margin Return on Inventory: profit earned per unit of money tied up on the shelf.
Slow mover  30% margin · 2 turns / year0.86
Fast mover  20% margin · 6 turns / year1.50
Lower margin, higher return: this opportunity cost is what a slower competitor product costs the shelf.
Illustrative figures · GMROI = margin × turns at cost ÷ (1 − margin)

Strategy 3 · Psychology
Behavioural Nudge Matrix: design the choice, not the argument
BiasTechniqueVisual cueClinicPharmacy
Status quo bias
Defaulting to familiar treatments out of habit
Loss-aversion reframing. Present staying with the older competitor treatment as the risk of remaining with an outdated option.
Anchoring
The first option shapes how the rest are judged
Choice architecture. Structure order packages so the middle package, the real target, reads as the best value.
Hidden signals
Skepticism, hesitation or sudden interest
Micro-expression analysis. Record role-play visits with actors and review them in slow motion to read split-second cues.
Guardrail: every frame must rest on approved, evidence-based product information and the local code of promotional practice.
Deliverable: Behavioural Nudge Matrix, mapping each bias to a framing technique for clinics and pharmacies

Strategy 6 · Toolkit
Sample Mega-Prompt: the skeptical university professor
Role & Persona Assignment: You are playing the role of Dr. [Doctor's Name], a Skeptical University Professor and Senior Consultant in Cardiology running a high-volume, time-constrained clinic.
Persona Traits & Behavioral Rules: - You are highly analytical, deeply skeptical of standard sales pitches, and protective of your time. - You value rigorous clinical trial endpoints, real-world safety data, and mechanism-of-action details over marketing claims. - If I attempt a traditional, pushy sales pitch, react with skepticism or disinterest. Only engage if I use tactical empathy, address potential objections upfront, or ask calibrated open questions.
Simulation Workflow: 1. Begin in character with a brief, time-pressured greeting as I enter your office. 2. Respond dynamically based on the clinical brochures and papers in your knowledge base. 3. Raise realistic objections about side-effect profiles, pricing, or head-to-head trial limitations. 4. Remain strictly in character until I type the command: EVALUATE_PITCH
Post-Simulation Evaluation Protocol: Once triggered by EVALUATE_PITCH, step out of character and score my pitch 1–10 on: 1. Scientific Accuracy 2. Conciseness 3. Linking Product to Benefits 4. Time Management 5. Tone of Influence, with brief actionable feedback for each.
